# first release scope

## supported product

- macOS 14 and later; notarized direct download; menu bar presence without a Dock icon.
- background activation with configurable `⌃⌥G` capture and `⌃⌥R` start/stop hotkeys. settings warn when a shortcut is unverified and ask the user to test it from another app; receiving the shortcut clears that warning. selection opens when record is pressed before choosing a target.
- full-screen selection overlay with display, selected-window, and free-region modes. keep its controls at the bottom of the screen's visible frame so they stay clear of the camera housing and Dock. capture one monitor at a time. regions can be moved by dragging inside them and resized by dragging a corner before recording; the corner magnifier appears during region creation and corner resizing, but not while moving a region. the last selection is restored when valid; reselect is immediate.
- record at an 60 fps capture target. capture the cursor by default, with a setting to disable it. show a thin, high-contrast outline just outside a selected region or window, with a small `recording` label at its top edge. adapt it to light and dark backgrounds and keep it out of the GIF. keep normal Mac interaction available while recording. follow a selected window as it moves or resizes.
- if a selected window moves to another display, keep following it. if it is minimized or closed, stop recording with a clear message and keep usable captured frames as a partial result. if there are no usable frames, show an error and clean up.
- if a selected display disconnects during recording, stop with a clear message and keep usable captured frames as a partial result. if there are no usable frames, show an error and clean up.
- stop with the same hotkey. show a floating, animated preview in a native window, initially 560 points wide, with height fitted once to all ready-preview components and then locked. allow width resizing; scroll when the screen cannot fit the content. let the preview use the content width within the window padding, scale the GIF proportionally within a 280 point height limit, and place it above trim, speed and fps menus and segmented loop controls on one row, and compression settings. keep file size, duration, and dimensions together above centered copy, save, and close actions. during GIF replacement, show one spinner and `updating gif` over the preview, keep the current preview and metadata visible, disable copy and save, and leave close available. do not show a completion message. `copy` (`⌘C`) copies GIF image data; `copy as file` (`⌘⇧C`) copies a retained GIF file attachment. both confirm success; `⌘S` opens the save sheet above the preview. closing an unexported preview asks before discarding; the confirmation offers explicit discard and keep-preview actions.
- trim the beginning and end before export with two timeline handles. choose playback speed from a 0.25×–3× menu in 0.25× steps, initially 1×. choose loop forever or once. preview and export must agree.
- while an edit is being encoded, keep the last usable preview visible and disable copy and save. apply only the newest requested edit. keep original frames and the original GIF for recovery; bound replacement outputs by the session quota and remove cancelled or obsolete outputs.
- compression uses the pinned Gifski 1.34.0 CLI locally. preserve the original; show compressed output with a back arrow beside copy. offer a quality slider from 1 to 100 in one-point steps, defaulting to 100, and original/75%/50%/25% resolution choices. changing resolution starts compression immediately; changing quality starts compression one second after scrubbing stops. these size and quality controls are independent of the compact `compress for` menu, which lists Discord (20 MB max) and GitHub (10 MB max); choosing a destination starts the size search. target 90% of the published cap using decimal megabytes (9 MB for GitHub image/GIF uploads; 18 MB for Discord non-Nitro attachments); treat this as an optimization target, not guaranteed acceptance. automatically search resolution, sampled frame rate, and Gifski quality for the smallest file under the target. the current resolution choice is the maximum search size; search that size and smaller choices only. the automatic search must keep Gifski quality at 90 or higher until the architect approves a lower floor; the quality slider remains 1–100. preserve the approved quality floor: text remains legible at native scale, cursor edges remain clear, and gradients have no distracting banding or flicker; minor differences visible only at 2× are acceptable. Gifski manages palette generation internally. do not show a size-against-target summary beneath the controls. do not bundle third-party destination marks unless written permission is obtained. the app embeds the pinned CLI and its complete notices; direct distribution remains gated on corresponding-source/build materials and final signing/notarization verification.
- guided screen-recording permission setup; clear failures for missing permissions, hotkey conflict, unavailable display/window, full disk, encoder failure, and failed clipboard or save operations.
- retain temporary completed media while its preview is active. remove it when the preview closes after export or after confirmed discard; remove orphaned preview media on the next launch after a crash without touching exported files. keep the latest file-copy attachment separately until the next file copy.
- subtle optional interaction sounds, brief motion feedback, reduced-motion support, keyboard navigation, and accessible labels.
- the unused space behind the preview contains a subtle, continuously animated dithered pattern. it is decorative, may show through transparent GIF pixels, and becomes still with reduced motion or low power mode; it is not part of the exported GIF.

## acceptance journeys

| journey | required observable outcome |
| --- | --- |
| first launch | permission guide appears when needed; menu bar app remains usable after permission is granted or refused. |
| region capture | user creates a region, moves it by dragging inside, and resizes it by dragging a corner; the corner magnifier assists creation and corner resizing but stays hidden while moving; only the selected pixels are encoded. |
| display and window capture | one display or one window is selected; moving or resizing the window keeps the recording attached to it. |
| background use | other apps receive normal mouse and keyboard input while recording; the record hotkey still stops capture. |
| preview and export | stop opens an animated preview; metadata matches exported GIF; copy (`⌘C`) publishes GIF image data; copy as file (`⌘⇧C`) retains a byte-identical attachment after preview closure and pastes animation into GitHub on a real Mac; save and `⌘S` produce the same valid file. |
| editing | two accessible timeline handles trim the beginning and end; the speed menu starts at 1× with 0.25×–3× presets in 0.25× steps; the fps menu starts at 50 with 50/45/30/24/20/15/10 presets; loop forever and once changes appear in the preview and persist in copied and saved GIFs. exports are unavailable until the newest edit is ready; a cancelled or failed edit leaves the previous preview usable, and the original frames and GIF remain available. |
| compression | original is recoverable with the back arrow; resolution, sampled frame rate, and quality controls alter the result; every accepted size-goal output meets the approved quality floor. |
| failure recovery | permission loss, disappearing target, low disk space, encoder failure, and hotkey conflict produce actionable states and clean up temporary files. after a crash, unfinished recordings are deleted on next launch rather than restored. |

## release boundary

- no account, cloud upload, screen-content analytics, video export, or captured audio.
- recording spans one monitor at a time. cross-display region recording is outside this release.
- no Slack size-goal button until a useful target is defined. no restoration of unfinished recordings after a crash.
- no promise of a specific size, frame rate, or latency until the benchmark environment and approved quality floor have been measured.

## destination limit policy

- source limits must be verified from each destination's official documentation before release and periodically afterward. show the exact scope of a limit, such as file attachment versus repository file.
- 2026-10-24 research snapshot: Discord's non-Nitro attachment FAQ lists 20 mb and notes experiments may give selected users higher limits; GitHub's issue, pull request, and comment image/GIF attachments are limited to 10 mb. scope, account conditions, and official mark-use sources are recorded in [the destination limits research note](../Benchmarks/destination-limits-2026-10-24.md). these are dated facts, not permanent product constants. [Slack's standard file upload limit is 1 gb](https://slack.com/help/articles/201330736-Add-files-to-Slack), so the architect excluded its preset until a useful size goal is defined.
